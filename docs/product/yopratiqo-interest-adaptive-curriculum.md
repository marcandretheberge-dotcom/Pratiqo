Updated November 27, 2026 (America/Toronto).

## 1. Product decision

The learner studies the language **through subjects they want to discover**.
Their selected interests shape substantial material throughout the whole
12-module parcours: the main texts, listening and educational video, vocabulary,
comprehension activities, and speaking/writing tasks.

A history-oriented Spanish learner should discover people, places, objects and
events in countries where Spanish is spoken. A learner selecting sports and
music should discover athletes, sporting cultures, competitions, instruments,
genres, performers and their contexts. The history of a genre is music content;
the history of a competition is sports content. Neither requires selecting
general history.

The language goals remain coherent across these choices. Essential vocabulary,
grammar prerequisites, communicative abilities and assessment expectations are
shared. Topic vocabulary, examples, information and the work done with them can
differ substantially. Shared grammar can be taught inside a selected subject;
it does not require most of the learner's lesson to remain generic.

Every module must contain a substantial selected-interest learning sequence.
A changed venue name, an optional directions mission, or a fact box appended to
an unchanged course is insufficient. All eight languages remain in scope;
Spanish is the first complete implementation target.

## 3. University references and their limits

The University of Michigan's [Spanish 232 Special Topics](https://sites.google.com/umich.edu/spanish232specialtopics/home)
offers thematically different routes serving the same language requirement and
intended proficiency. Its [Fall 2026 courses](https://sites.google.com/umich.edu/spanish232specialtopics/current-courses)
include language and culture through Bad Bunny's music and a Latin American rock
course using readings, videos, songs, films and podcasts. Its
[departmental sequence](https://lsa.umich.edu/rll/undergraduates/course-information/elp-course-descriptions.html)
also integrates cultural subjects and thematic language functions in earlier
Spanish courses.

The University of Texas at Austin's [Spanish Proficiency Exercises](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/spe/)
provides a useful support pattern: a simpler scripted clip, natural speaker
examples, vocabulary, sample phrases, grammar and transcript/translation access.

These are precedents for teaching language through substantive subjects and
making media accessible. They do not validate an automatic personalization
algorithm, a 70/30 formula, or a twelve-module B1 guarantee. YoPratiqo must author
its own material and assessments and establish rights for published media.

The Council of Europe describes the [CEFR as non-prescriptive](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/introduction-and-context).
Its [spoken-performance framework](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-3-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-qualitative-aspects-of-spoken-language-use)
considers range, accuracy, fluency, interaction and coherence. This is why a
grammar checklist or a self-reported starting point cannot establish proficiency.

## 4. Common progression, variable content

| Common curriculum contract | Selected-interest material |
| --- | --- |
| Communicative goals and prerequisite sequence | Factual subject, people, places, objects and events |
| Core grammatical concepts and essential general vocabulary | Thematic vocabulary encountered in the main input |
| Comparable listening, reading, interaction and production demands | Main reading, audio, instructional video and contextual examples |
| Assessment criteria and required opportunities to use a skill | Comprehension questions, speaking missions and writing tasks |
| Stable progression and equivalent practice slots | Immutable versions of the actual prompts, answers and media |

Keep facts inside the learning input. Questions assess understanding of that
input and use of its language, not the learner's prior sporting or historical
knowledge. A later task should transfer the language to a fresh comparable
situation. A successful quiz is evidence of that performance, not automatic
mastery of an entire tense.

### Selecting and changing interests

- Retain `music`, `art`, `history`, `sport`, `food` and `nature`, with up to three
  selections per language. With no interests selected, use a balanced general
  course.
- Use a primary subject on each content package plus supporting facets such as
  origins, geography, community, technology or a historical event. A music
  package with an origins facet remains eligible for a music-only learner.
- Select from published packages that satisfy the current language objectives,
  prerequisite and media requirements. Balance all selected interests across
  upcoming lessons while retaining enough continuity to reuse vocabulary.
- Preserve all six preference choices during rollout, but disclose which have
  published main-course coverage in that language. Enable the full-course
  personalization promise only for supported selections; offer an explicit
  general-course fallback for unavailable material. Do not silently ignore a
  chosen interest or call an unchanged fallback personalized.
- Do not hard-code a founder-mandated percentage. Track meaningful coverage
  and avoid allowing one selected interest to disappear from the course.
- Freeze a lesson's material when it begins. Preference changes affect future
  lessons; completed work, unfinished answers and due reviews retain their
  exact content versions. Make any deliberate restart clear to the learner.
- Brief geographic or social context may support any subject. Unrelated
  general-history passages should not dominate a sports-and-music course.

## 5. What an interest-based lesson contains

Each package names a substantive question or discovery, its source and the
factual takeaways. The learner must learn something about the selected subject;
a generic practical scenario alone cannot fulfil the package. This applies to
scheduling, services and travel modules as much as to overt historical stories.

1. **Discover:** a short source-backed text, listening passage or educational
   video teaches something about the chosen subject.
2. **Understand:** the learner identifies meaning, relationships or a sequence
   from that material, with level-appropriate support.
3. **Notice language:** useful words and a grammatical function are explained
   through the same material, alongside necessary general vocabulary.
4. **Use it:** the learner describes, compares, asks, explains or narrates in a
   meaningful writing/speaking task.
5. **Return to it:** later recall and a new application reuse language actually
   encountered. Missed or helped work remains distinguishable from independent
   performance.

Beginners can study intellectually interesting subjects through brief captions,
maps, concrete objects, a few clear facts and supported audio. Historical themes
do not require demanding an untaught past-tense narrative in module one.

Texts, audio and video must align. Provide transcripts, captions where relevant,
replay and vocabulary anchors. Educational video has instructional content;
Yoppo's existing travel/success cinema does not satisfy that role. Publish a
playable asset only when it exists and matches the script. Label illustrations
and invented practice scenarios accurately rather than implying archival footage
or current real-world schedules.

### Worked example: music and comparisons

The University of the Republic's [candombe material](https://www.eumus.edu.uy/candombe/datasets/ISMIR2015/candombe.html)
describes three drum types and their different sizes. A short original beginner
adaptation could read:

> En el candombe hay tres tipos de tambor: chico, repique y piano. El piano es
> más grande que el chico.

This is proposed lesson copy, not an existing recording. A learner identifies
the instruments, learns `tambor`, `grande`, `pequeño` and `tamaño`, understands
`más grande que`, and compares two instruments in a short spoken description.
Here `piano` names a drum. A later lesson can revisit the tradition's community
and historical context while remaining a music-interest lesson.

## 6. Spanish: proposed coverage across all twelve modules

The existing Spanish course contains twelve units with three lessons each. The
matrix preserves its actual sequence: completed past actions are explicit in
unit 11 and future plans with `ir a + infinitive` in unit 12. Gemini's earlier
past/subjunctive sequence is a separate curriculum redesign, not a drop-in overlay.

**These are proposed content packages, not completed or approved lessons.**
Each needs a source packet, adapted script, activities and matching media.
Practical language remains available within a substantial factual discovery.
The mixed-interest column illustrates a profile selecting both sport and music;
each should also work independently when selected alone.

| Module and language goal | History-oriented material | Sports-and-music material | Application using the material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Identity, origin, current place, spelling and clarification | Illustrated places and collections in Mexico and Peru; labels identify country and community | Verified musician and athlete profiles from different Spanish-speaking countries | Match person/place/country, distinguish origin from current location, spell a name and request repetition |
| 2. Preferences, quantities, ordering and payment | A museum-sourced introduction to cacao/chocolate, followed by a contemporary café situation | Discover bachata instruments and sound, followed by a break at a rehearsal or sporting event | Extract items and quantities, express a preference, make and correct an order using essential general vocabulary |
| 3. Location, directions and verification | A simplified Qhapaq Ñan map and the places it connects | Candombe neighbourhoods in Montevideo, or a sourced sporting venue and district | Locate features, use `hay/está`, give directions and check the destination on a teaching map |
| 4. Time, duration, schedules and changes | Discover a heritage institution or celebration, then read an invented visit programme | Discover a rehearsal, festival or club gathering, then read an invented activity programme | Distinguish start time from duration, arrange a meeting and explain a change |
| 5. Quantities, prices, comparisons and reasons | Products and exchange in a documented Andean or Mexican context, followed by a modern market task | Instruments or equipment through an actual workshop/club explanation | Extract material vocabulary, compare options, explain a choice and retain unit/weight/price language |
| 6. Services, permission and problem-solving | How a historic site or collection is conserved and visited | How a rehearsal space or sports facility works | Understand a rule, ask about a service, describe a problem and request a solution for an imagined visit |
| 7. People, relationships, descriptions and invitations | A documented group of contemporary heritage workers and their roles | A band, dance group or team, including women and community participants | Identify people, use possessives and adjective agreement, introduce someone and invite them |
| 8. Transport, destinations and interruptions | Why places connect through a sourced transport-history account | Touring, away games or travel between cultural events | Read an itinerary, understand a change and request another departure with `tengo que/puedo` |
| 9. Work, ordered instructions and clarification | How a museum/archaeology collection is documented | Behind a performance or match: an instrument maker, technician, organiser or coach | Explain roles, order instructions with `primero/después`, identify a missing item and ask a precise question |
| 10. Routines, tastes, weather and conditions | Everyday heritage work or exploring historic places | Practice/training routines and community music-making | Distinguish routine from an exception, express tastes and agree on a weather-dependent plan |
| 11. Completed past, narrative and reactions | A sourced historical episode or museum discovery | Uruguay's 1930 World Cup or a sourced episode in a genre's history | Order events, distinguish fact from reaction and retell a short sequence with supported past forms |
| 12. Plans, constraints and a full exchange | Plan a heritage trip or small exhibition using earlier discoveries | Plan a music-and-sports itinerary, programme or community event | Compare plans, explain a choice, negotiate time/budget, handle a change and confirm the arrangement |

Useful initial source anchors: [Qhapaq Ñan, UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1459/),
[candombe, UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/es/RL/el-candombe-y-su-espacio-sociocultural-una-practica-comunitaria-00182),
[Dominican bachata, UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/music-and-dance-of-dominican-bachata-01514),
[1930 World Cup, FIFA](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/uruguay-1930),
and [Templo Mayor, INAH](https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/4463).
These establish subject matter, not permission to copy entire media assets.
Teaching Inca or Mexica history in Spanish must not imply those societies
originally spoke Spanish or that their communities exist only in the past.

## 7. Onboarding and the speaking mission

Use the existing experience/goal/pace preferences where possible rather than
adding a second competing questionnaire. The following is a proposed flow,
not shipped UX or proof of a measured completion time.

| Step | Learner-facing purpose | Required behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | “Are you starting fresh, returning to Spanish, or already using it?” | Self-report offers an appropriate start or the existing diagnostic. Do not label the learner A2/B1, mark skipped modules complete or grant access solely from this answer. |
| Interests | “What would you enjoy discovering in Spanish?” | Show history, music, sport, art, food and nature; allow the existing three selections and a general option. Explain that choices shape main course materials. |
| Speaking situations | “Where would you like to use Spanish?” | Reuse purpose preferences where available. Teach context- and region-appropriate address; do not equate all locals with `tú` or all clients/hosts with `usted`. |
| Preview and begin | Show the selected mix and one actual upcoming discovery | Use the published lesson title and available media. Preserve the learner's current lesson if returning; keep interests editable. |

A priority/secondary screen is unnecessary for the first implementation. If
later added, secondary interests still participate in the main course rather
than being confined to review. Register and region preferences should guide
examples without removing useful alternatives or promising unsupported tutor
matching.

### End-of-module mission

Give each module a concrete speaking mission grounded in its factual input,
language objectives and learned words. Offer a small phrase sheet and an
optional private rehearsal. A history learner can explain a discovery; a
music learner can compare instruments or introduce a genre; a sports learner
can recount an event once the required past language has been taught.

“Practice with a Pratiqo tutor” should lead into real tutor availability and
booking. Interest overlap is a useful filter only when tutors have provided
that information. Do not manufacture three matching tutors, their interests,
availability, session durations or regional expertise.

The learner must be able to preview and choose what is shared for that booking:
selected subject, actual completed module and content version, practiced
objectives, relevant vocabulary, summarized difficulties, speaking mission and
suggested prompts. Distinguish “practiced” from “mastered.” Keep the existing
summary-only boundary: private voice notes, transcripts and detailed quiz answers
are not part of this brief.

Reuse the existing authenticated, booking-specific teacher bridge and its
consent/retention contract. Do not place the brief in a public ranking profile.
After a session, teacher observations may propose relevant practice with clear
provenance. Course continuation remains available independently of booking;
teacher feedback does not silently award course completion, change entitlements
or manufacture verified XP.

## 8. Content model and integration requirements

Gemini's shared curriculum/overlay separation is useful as a draft. Its supplied
schema is not ready to serve as the runtime contract: the single-unit sample is
an excerpt and does not meet its own twelve-unit minimum; overlays can omit
units; repeated unit IDs and broken cross-references are not ruled out by the
shown constraints; and every quiz type requires answer options. It lacks the
substantive text/video/source model, exact activity revisions and reward mapping
needed by the existing app. Do not deploy that JSON unchanged.

| Contract | Required information |
| --- | --- |
| Curriculum unit | Existing stable unit ID, objectives, prerequisites, core vocabulary, assessment criteria and equivalent practice-slot IDs |
| Content package | Immutable package ID and version; unit/lesson references; primary interest and supporting facets; target language/region; factual question and takeaways with sources; original learning inputs; thematic vocabulary; exact activities/answer definitions and speaking task |
| Educational resource | Text/audio/video type, source and rights information, exact script/transcript, captions and vocabulary anchors, asset identity, publication/readiness state |
| Learner plan | Selected interests, resolver version, upcoming coverage and pinned versions of begun lessons; preserve existing progress identities |
| Attempt and review | Exact package/activity revision, submitted answer, assistance and outcome; review retains the material actually encountered |
| Reward equivalence | Server-owned mapping from exact graded variant to an equivalent practice slot; preserve existing award history |
| Tutor brief | Booking-specific selected content and observed progress, original mission, exact learner-approved fields and the existing consent lifecycle |

Use type-specific activity definitions: free recall cannot be represented solely
as multiple choice, and open speaking should not pretend to have one selectable
correct answer. Keep vocabulary exposure and skill evidence distinct. A rule
ID can group observations, but one missed item is not a diagnosis and one
passed item is not general mastery.

Publication validation must check all twelve units, unique IDs, matching map
keys, referenced rules/words/answers, actual objective coverage, source/media
readiness and balanced available interests. Reference integrity and curriculum
coverage need semantic checks in addition to structural JSON validation.
Remove hard-coded 70/30 constants and do not constrain the shared architecture
to Spanish/English-only fields. Adapt target-language scripts and explanations
to the app's existing language and interface contracts.

### Existing repository boundaries

- `lib/yo/interests/preferences.ts` and `content.ts` currently select optional
  practice. `components/yo/interests/InterestPracticeCard.tsx` renders that
  mini-mission. Neither resolves the main course through interests.
- `lib/yo/spanish/courseData.ts` assembles fixed lesson and assessment files.
  `lib/yo/personalized/courseData.ts` does not implement course-wide thematic
  selection either.
- `lib/yo/review/courseRegistry.ts`, `courseContent.server.ts` and
  `lib/yo/rankings/grade.server.ts` resolve canonical authored activities. New
  displayed answers under an old activity ID would break this contract.
- `lib/yo/foundations/unitSession.ts` already has activity/unit revisions;
  preserve draft and resume behaviour while adding immutable variant identity.
- Exact variants must map server-side to **equivalent practice slots**, not a
  single reward for every exercise sharing a broad grammatical goal. Switching
  themes must not multiply the same slot's XP; distinct genuine exercises must
  not all lose their rewards either. The client cannot select the grouping.
- `lib/yo/foundations/audio.server.ts` resolves exact locale/text recordings.
  Italian, German, Portuguese, Japanese and Mandarin also have separate audio
  registries. New scripts require their own matching assets.
- `lib/yo/italian/course/courseTypes.ts` needs a structured educational-resource
  contract. `components/yo/travel/useMissionCinema.ts` remains separate.
- Reuse one curriculum resolver with adapters for the shared courses, Italian's
  guided player, Japanese's reading aids and Mandarin's characters/pinyin/tones.
- `app/api/bookings/[id]/yopratiqo-bridge/route.ts` and
  `lib/yopratiqo/teacherBridge.ts` provide the existing tutor-sharing boundary.
  Add interests and exact missions through a versioned extension.

## 9. Completion criteria and delivery order

The first complete increment is Spanish across all twelve modules, with history,
music and sport packages demonstrating both single-interest and mixed-interest
paths. Author the remaining three existing interests and extend the common
contracts through all eight languages. This sequencing is not a claim that the
other languages or interests are already adapted.

Completion requires:

- Main inputs, thematic vocabulary and activities genuinely differ between
  history and sports/music profiles in every module, while required language
  goals remain comparable. Every package identifies a factual discovery and
  source, including modules that also practise everyday transactions.
- All selected interests recur meaningfully; changing choices preserves begun
  work, completed progress, exact reviews and legitimate XP.
- An authored modality plan delivers reading and listening throughout each
  complete twelve-module interest path, together with planned educational-video
  sequences. Every lesson need not use all three formats, but a text-only release
  does not complete the requested multimedia path.
- Educational media is real, source-backed and matched to its transcript and
  exercises. Published course coverage accurately describes which text, audio
  and video exist; illustrative plans do not count as delivered assets. The
  onboarding promise matches the interests actually covered in that language.
- Grading resolves the exact authored variant. Assessments establish language
  use from supplied material, with subsequent transfer, without prior trivia
  knowledge being necessary.
- The immediate lesson reflects onboarding choices and the end-of-unit speaking
  mission uses the material actually studied.
- Tutor practice and sharing remain optional and use real availability and the
  existing booking/consent boundaries.

Write and review the full Spanish content map before the next large recording
batch. Preserve useful shared recordings; identify the exact new scripts before
paying to produce audio/video. A small end-to-end implementation can establish
the integration contract, but does not close this workstream until coverage spans
the whole twelve-module course.

No application behaviour, media generation or proficiency claim is delivered by
this documentation correction.
